# Working With Zara

A personal user manual to help us work well together. It covers how I work, what I value and what you can expect from me. Everyone works differently, so treat this as the start of a conversation.

*Last updated: November 2026*

---

## 01 | About me

I am based in Ottawa, Canada, and have lived in Dubai and Halifax. I studied at Saint Mary's University and Dalhousie University and have spent over 15 years in technology, across business operations, client success, digital analytics and leadership.

The short version of my job: I help businesses solve problems and build better ways of working, so people and technology deliver results together. I am a builder and an intrapreneur, and I am most interested in where business, technology, AI and automation meet.

Outside work I spend time with my family, build personal technology projects, do strength training, travel and follow sports.

**Where I can help you**

- **Strategy and ambiguity:** connecting dots and giving structure to undefined problems.
- **Operations:** simplifying processes and finding another route when the standard one fails.
- **Business and technology:** translating technical capability into practical solutions.
- **Coaching and stakeholders:** competing priorities, difficult conversations and complex relationships.
- **Innovation:** turning ideas into working solutions.
- **Stretching beyond your usual box:** pushing you out of your comfort zone and helping you work outside your typical role.

If you are working through a challenge in one of these areas, use my experience.

## 02 | What you can expect from me

I want everyone I lead to have three things:

- **Trust and autonomy.** Use your judgment, make decisions and own your work without micromanagement.
- **Growth.** You should leave more capable and confident than you arrived, beyond what your current role requires.
- **Psychological safety.** You can challenge me, ask questions, admit mistakes and say "I don't know." You do not need to agree with me to work well with me.

My commitments to you:

- I will support your development and advocate for your career, including when you are not in the room.
- I will give honest, constructive feedback, and credit where it is due.
- I will remove obstacles and explain the reasoning behind decisions.
- I will back thoughtful experiments and calculated risks.
- I will say what I know, what I don't know and what I cannot share.
- I will respect your time, individuality and boundaries.
- I will own my mistakes and hold myself to the standards I ask of you.

## 03 | How I work

- **I am a question person.** I get to answers through questions. I ask many of them and challenge assumptions, my own included. It is how I think and says nothing about whether I trust you. Over time with me, expect book recommendations on asking better questions, starting with *Change Your Questions, Change Your Life*.
- **I ask why first.** Before we invest time in something, I want to know its purpose and what success looks like. The question I start with: "What problem are we solving, and how will we know it worked?"
- **I look past this week.** I consider long-term effects, so I may raise things that feel early.
- **I measure results over activity.** Being busy and being effective are different things.
- **I want a range of views.** My decisions improve after debate.
- **I challenge the status quo.** If you prefer things to stay as they are, working with me may feel uncomfortable. If you enjoy finding better ways to work, we will get along well.

Principles I work by:

1. Own the outcome, not just the task.
2. Be curious before critical.
3. Progress over perfection.
4. Automate the repetitive and spend human effort where it adds value.
5. Leave things better than you found them.
6. Individual success should add to collective success. My motto: all of us are smarter than one of us.

## 04 | Trust and autonomy

Trust builds through consistent action over time. Three behaviours stand out to me:

- **Be proactive.** Anticipate needs and raise risks before they become problems.
- **Own the outcome.** Check whether your work achieved its purpose once the task is done.
- **Invest in your development.** Build your knowledge, skills and judgment, and put them to work. I do this myself: I share what I learn with my clients and colleagues and apply it in my own work.

I don't expect you to have every answer. I expect you to be curious and resourceful.

I adjust my involvement to your experience and track record. Once I see you building momentum and making steady progress, I step back. The aim is for you to make decisions independently.

## 05 | Mistakes

I don't expect perfection. I expect thoughtful decisions and accountability. When something goes wrong I look at three things:

- **Pattern:** is this the first time, or a recurring issue?
- **Judgment:** was it a calculated risk or a lack of care?
- **Due diligence:** did you gather enough information and check your assumptions before acting?

I support experimentation, including when it fails. Repeating the same mistake without learning from it, or skipping due diligence, concerns me. I judge the quality of the decision process as well as the outcome.

## 06 | Communication, getting help and hours

I prefer live conversation for complex topics, brainstorming and sensitive matters. For routine communication I value clarity, relevance and timeliness.

- **Preferred channel:** Call me for anything urgent or important. A casual chat or a brainstorm is always welcome. Use email for matters that do not need my immediate attention.
- **Typical response time:** My team is my priority. You are on my priority list, so expect a response as soon as I am available.
- **Urgent matters:** Ping me with the word "urgent" and we will talk right away. For these situations you will have access to channels reserved for my team.
- **Bad news:** Deliver good news fast and bad news faster. Over time you will see that this protocol earns trust, and it gets easier the longer we work together. It is my job to be transparent and set that example, so feel comfortable doing the same.
- **Decisions:** The test is whether it can be undone. Reversible decisions in your area are yours: decide, then tell me what and why. Decisions that are hard to undo, or that affect clients or other teams, we make together: bring options and a recommendation, and expect questions from me. What I decide myself depends on your team and role, so ask me in one of our 1:1s and I will share the details. Once we decide, we commit, including when we disagreed. As you build momentum, more decisions move to you.

**When you bring me a problem,** it helps to share:

- What is happening and why it matters.
- What you know and what is still uncertain.
- What you have checked so far.
- Options and their trade-offs.
- Where you need my input.

You do not need all of that to ask for help. If you have been stuck for a few hours, come to me as you are and we will work through it together.

**Hours.** I work Eastern Time business hours in Ottawa. I value outcomes over hours online and support flexibility, provided commitments are met and availability is communicated. If a deadline is at risk, tell me early so we can adjust priorities and find options together.

## 07 | Meetings and 1:1s

Meetings should earn their place on the calendar. A good one has a clear purpose and agenda, prepared participants, room to debate, and decisions with owners and deadlines. If we can get the same result asynchronously, we should.

Use our 1:1s for:

- Priorities and challenges.
- Decisions and support you need.
- Career development and aspirations.
- Feedback in both directions.
- Workload and well-being.
- Ideas beyond your immediate responsibilities.
- A little humor, and the occasional update on how awesome the Habs are.

They are your time.

## 08 | Feedback

I believe we give feedback because we care. When feedback stops, it is a sign that care has stopped too.

I give feedback that is direct, timely, specific and actionable, tailored to the person's experience and circumstances. I give constructive feedback in private and recognise contributions in public. I don't wait for formal reviews.

Feedback goes both ways. If something about my approach is not working for you, tell me when it happens.

## 09 | Things you might misread

| What you might observe | What I want you to know |
|---|---|
| I communicate directly. | I can be direct and empathetic at the same time. |
| I challenge your ideas. | I am challenging the thinking and respect the person. |
| I have high expectations. | I want you to realise your potential. |
| I ask many questions. | Curiosity is how I learn, and it does not signal distrust. |
| I give you autonomy. | I am still available and interested. |
| I show up with a lot of passion. | It means I care. The timeline and the work are still yours to own. |
| I show urgency. | I don't expect you to match my pace. |
| I show up in the details of your work. | I am interested in the day-to-day. The work stays yours. |

A client once described me to my manager this way: "She is ready to launch a spaceship to Mars, and she wants to take all of you with her." The second half is the part I care about most.

If my intent and my impact differ, tell me. Good intentions do not replace self-awareness.

## 10 | What gives me energy, and what I do when it is missing

I am energised by:

- Watching people grow past their own expectations.
- Solving complex business problems and turning ideas into working solutions.
- Learning something new and applying it to a real problem.
- Working with curious, motivated people who bring different expertise.
- Building scalable solutions with measurable impact.
- Celebrating achievements and recognising contributions.

When the conditions below show up, I raise them directly:

- **Unclear ownership:** I ask who owns it.
- **Repetitive manual work:** I ask whether it can be improved or automated.
- **Undefined objectives:** I ask what success looks like.
- **Commitments without a realistic delivery plan:** I ask what it will take to deliver.
- **Information withheld or shaded:** I ask for the full picture.
- **Change resisted because "we've always done it this way":** I ask what would have to be true for us to change.
- **Individual wins at the expense of the group:** I name the trade-off.
- **Silence:** I value proactive communication over silence. When I ask for ideas, share them, and do not wait to be asked before raising your own questions and ideas.

I treat each of these as something to fix together.

## 11 | What I'm working on

I am working on being less hands-on in execution, so I can spend more time on strategic direction, planning and developing the team. I enjoy solving problems and building, so I need to be deliberate about when I step in and when I make space for others to lead.

You can help by taking ownership, reporting progress, using your judgment and telling me where you need support. I am genuinely interested in the day-to-day, and that interest does not mean I want to do the work. If my presence or engagement ever feels like I am taking it from you, tell me and I will step back. A confident "I've got this" is all I need to hear.

I want to build teams that succeed because they are capable and empowered.
